abstract: >-
  Creating visual effects (VFX) for 3D content requires specialized design knowledge and technical expertise. Existing methods focus on creating VFX for videos or exploring designs for novices, while professional workflows need frameworks producing complete parameterized 3D assets. This study presents SpeLL, a generative framework that produces editable 3D VFX drafts from text prompts using large language models and retrieval-based asset assembly. A formative study with professional VFX designers identified three design requirements: preference for simplified prompting, need for VFX-specialized assets, and manual spatial-temporal coordination. SpeLL addresses these through prompt enhancement, a spatial design module for component retrieval and layout arrangement, and a temporal design module for activation sequencing. A user study with twenty professional designers evaluated SpeLL against ablated frameworks, 3D generation baselines, and manual workflows. Results showed SpeLL scored higher on intention alignment, spatial-temporal layout quality, and editability, and reduced the effort needed to reach editable VFX structures. Participants valued parameterized outputs over visual complexity, emphasizing workflow integration and manual refinement. These findings indicate that combining LLM reasoning with retrieval-based asset assembly can support early exploration and drafting in professional VFX workflows, while leaving final expressive control to designers.
